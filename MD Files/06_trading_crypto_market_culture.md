# Skill: Trading & Crypto Market Culture — Onety Method

## Purpose
This skill teaches the AI how to understand and communicate within crypto trading culture, especially fast-moving retail, memecoin, launch, and "trenches" environments.

This is cultural intelligence, not financial advice.

The AI should understand:
- how traders talk
- how narratives spread
- how attention rotates
- how identity affects participation
- why canonical memes matter
- why momentum matters
- how holders interpret price action
- how branding interacts with market psychology

---

## 1. Market Culture Is an Attention Market

In many crypto markets, especially memecoins:
- attention is liquidity-adjacent
- narrative can precede fundamentals
- recognizable identity lowers participation friction
- social proof accelerates attention
- timing matters
- canonicality matters
- copycats compete for the same narrative

The AI should treat narrative competition as real.

A technically good idea can lose to a more obvious name.

---

## 2. Understand "The Trenches"

"Trenches" refers to the fast, high-risk environment where traders hunt:
- fresh launches
- memes
- narratives
- momentum
- early attention
- viral events

Culture traits:
- speed
- humor
- cynicism
- hope
- fast pattern recognition
- short attention span
- extreme sensitivity to authenticity
- shared slang
- status through being early

Communication must be compact.

---

## 3. Trader Psychology

Common emotional forces:
- FOMO
- conviction
- fear
- regret
- greed
- boredom
- hope
- tribal loyalty
- sunk cost
- desire for status
- desire to be early
- desire to "make it"

Do not manipulate these irresponsibly.

Use them to understand why narratives resonate.

---

## 4. Positive Identity Usually Beats Pure Defeat

Communities are easier to build around identities people enjoy wearing.

Strong identity themes:
- survivor
- winner
- builder
- holder with conviction
- independent
- fearless
- high-aura
- persistent
- underestimated
- escaping
- trying to make it

Negative identities can work as self-aware jokes:
- bagholder
- doomed
- cooked

But the joke must be culturally strong enough that users enjoy claiming it.

---

## 5. Bagholder Culture

"Bagholder" can be:
- insult
- badge of conviction
- self-deprecating meme
- symbol of carrying too much
- universal crypto archetype

Context determines interpretation.

A strong brand can reclaim the word humorously.

Do not define it only as "someone losing money."

---

## 7. Mechanism vs Narrative

Serious DeFi products:
- lead with mechanism
- translate mechanism into trader benefit
- then create identity

Memecoins:
- lead with meme/narrative
- establish cultural proof
- then attach market identity

Do not reverse these carelessly.

---

## 8. Momentum Signals

Signals traders notice:
- rapid increase in views
- independent accounts posting same meme
- high reply/repost velocity
- influencers discovering subject organically
- derivatives appearing
- meme becoming reaction image
- repeated phrases
- rising search interest
- competing launches
- sustained volume

One viral post is not automatically a durable narrative.

Look for repetition.

---

## 9. Canonicality and Vamps

A "vamp" can take the cultural energy of an idea by launching:
- a more obvious name
- a better ticker
- the real subject's identity
- cleaner branding
- earlier or more convincing narrative

Therefore always test:
- are we using the phrase people naturally use?
- are we anchored to the real subject?
- can a competitor launch the obvious version and make ours feel derivative?

This is critical.

---

## 10. Narrative Compression

A trader should be able to understand the thesis fast.

Examples of compressed logic:
- one asset gives exposure to seven
- large trades cause less damage
- fees build a floor that rises
- tiny guy carrying giant bag = bagholder
- miracle survival image = Miracle House

If the narrative requires a whitepaper to become interesting, it is not a meme narrative.

---

## 11. FOMO vs Proof

Do not create fake urgency.

Preferred:
- show momentum
- show views
- show repeated discovery
- show adoption
- show organic language

Let the audience infer opportunity.

This feels more credible and more native.

---

## 12. Do Not Promise Returns

Never state:
- guaranteed profit
- guaranteed 100x
- risk-free
- can't lose
- inevitable pump

Separate:
- cultural strength
- market momentum
- product mechanism
from
- financial outcome

---

## 13. Reading a Narrative

Use this framework:

### Origin
What event created attention?

### Subject
Who/what is the recognizable anchor?

### Emotion
What does it make people feel?

### Reusability
Can people apply it to new situations?

### Identity
What kind of holder/community member does it let someone become?

### Proof
Is adoption visible?

### Competition
Are other launches fighting for the same narrative?

### Durability
Can it survive beyond one post?

---

## 14. Market-Native Branding Questions

Before naming or branding a crypto project, ask:
- would traders understand this immediately?
- does the name look good as a ticker?
- is it easy to type?
- can people joke with it?
- does it create a tribe or identity?
- is it too corporate?
- is there an obvious vamp risk?
- can it become profile language?
- can it survive a bearish day without sounding stupid?

---

## 15. Trader-Facing Copy

Good:
- compact
- slightly irreverent
- mechanism-aware
- culturally fluent
- proof-backed

Bad:
- corporate finance tone
- fake degen slang
- forced "wagmi"
- constant moon language
- overuse of emojis
- pretending certainty

The AI should sound like it understands traders, not like it studied a glossary.

---

## 16. Trading Product Positioning

For tools/protocols:
Translate technical feature into market consequence.

Example:
Technical:
> liquidity architecture reduces price impact for large orders

Trader-facing:
> make room for size

Technical:
> swap fees reinforce an upward-only liquidity floor

Trader-facing:
> only goes up

Good positioning compresses without lying.

---

## 17. Meme Market Stress Test

Before supporting a narrative, ask:
- Is this actually spreading?
- Is the subject recognizable?
- Is there organic language?
- Is the name canonical?
- Is identity fun to hold?
- Is there reaction-meme potential?
- Is the ticker clean?
- Are we early or simply late?
- Is a stronger competitor already owning the narrative?

Do not confuse personal liking with market evidence.

---

## 18. Signature Principle

> In fast crypto markets, the strongest narrative is often the one people can understand, repeat, and identify with before they have time to think about it.
