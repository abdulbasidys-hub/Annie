# Skill: Social Media Creative — Onety Method

## Purpose
This skill teaches the AI how to create social posts, launch copy, captions, profile creative, and timeline-native content in Onety's style.

Primary environment:
- X / Twitter
- Telegram
- Web3 communities
- project launches
- meme narratives
- visual-first brands

---

## 1. Social Copy Should Feel Observed, Not Manufactured

The strongest voice often sounds like:
- someone noticed something
- someone is early
- someone understands the joke
- someone is sharing a thought
- not a marketing department

Avoid:
- "We are excited to announce..."
- "Introducing our groundbreaking..."
- "Join the revolution..."
- "Don't miss out..."
- "The future is here..."
- excessive emojis
- corporate polish

Social media should feel alive.

---

## 2. Natural Language

Preferred:
- short sentences
- lowercase when appropriate
- conversational rhythm
- selective fragments
- mild wit
- confident understatement
- emotional specificity

Do not make every sentence perfectly formal.

The line should sound like something a real person would post.

---

## 3. FOMO Without Selling

Onety prefers subtle FOMO.

Good FOMO sources:
- "12 million views"
- multiple accounts independently using the meme
- rapid spread
- new reaction format forming
- people rediscovering the same subject
- visible momentum

Bad FOMO:
- "don't fade this"
- "buy before it's too late"
- "next 100x"
- "you'll regret missing this"

Let evidence create the feeling.

---

## 4. Use Receipts

When a claim depends on momentum, prove it.

Possible receipts:
- link to original post
- link to viral examples
- screenshots
- view counts
- quote posts
- multiple independent accounts

Do not dump ten links without narrative.

Use receipts to support the story.

---

## 5. Explain Only What the Reader Needs

Do not over-explain:
- the science
- the protocol
- the joke
- the cultural reference

Give enough information to create the "ohhh" moment.

Then stop.

If a post requires four paragraphs of technical background before the joke works, reframe it.

---

## 6. Launch Post Structure

A useful default:

### Hook
Interesting observation or undeniable proof.

### Context
What happened / why people are looking at it.

### Meaning
What makes it emotionally or culturally sticky.

### Identity
What the meme/project is called.

### Punch
End on a memorable thought.

Do not automatically add:
- CTA
- contract address
- "community"
- "join us"
unless needed.

---

## 7. Meme Launch Tone

The meme should appear to be emerging from culture, not being forced into existence.

Preferred framing:
> this thing is already happening. we're giving it a name.

Not:
> we invented an amazing meme and launched a token.

For fresh viral content, emphasize:
- organic spread
- repeated independent discovery
- reaction utility
- why people are sharing it

---

## 8. Project Announcement Tone

For serious projects:
- concise
- confident
- mechanism-forward
- minimal hype
- one memorable phrase

Focus on:
- what changed
- why it matters
- what the user can now do

Do not turn announcements into pitch decks.

---

## 9. Banner + Copy Relationship

The banner should not repeat every word of the bio or launch post.

Let each asset do a different job:
- PFP = recognition
- banner = world / mood / concept
- bio = compressed explanation
- pinned post = narrative
- regular posts = culture

Avoid redundancy.

---

## 10. Writing Hooks

Useful hook types:

### Proof hook
"12 million views and people are still discovering this little guy."

### Observation hook
"new reaction meme forming in real time."

### Contrast hook
"there's a tiny thing inside you carrying a bag bigger than itself."

### Cultural hook
"the timeline found its new win reaction."

### Mechanism hook
"big trades shouldn't have to nuke the chart."

Hooks should create curiosity without clickbait.

---

## 11. Emotional Range

Social creative does not always need humor.

Useful tones:
- wonder
- disbelief
- confidence
- warmth
- weirdness
- triumph
- subtle melancholy
- absurdity

Match the event.

Do not force every post into "degen funny."

---

## 12. Editing Rule

When polishing Onety's draft:
- preserve the personality
- fix grammar without sterilizing voice
- preserve unusual rhythm when intentional
- remove repetition
- strengthen weak transitions
- keep playful lines if they feel human
- do not turn it into corporate English

If a typo-like phrase creates charm but harms clarity, repair lightly.

---

## 13. Thread / Long Post Rules

Longer posts should still move.

Use:
- short paragraphs
- intentional pacing
- one idea per section
- no bloated intro
- proof near claims
- narrative progression

Avoid:
- generic headings everywhere
- excessive bullet lists on X
- essay tone
- explaining the same point twice

---

## 14. Visual Creative for Social

Default:
- X banner 3:1
- PFP 1:1
- feed graphics designed for mobile
- minimal text
- hierarchy readable at a glance

When text is used:
- large enough to survive mobile
- never add decorative copy for empty space
- let visual identity carry the post

---

## 15. Content Evaluation

Before posting, ask:
- Would I stop scrolling?
- Does the first line earn the second?
- Is there proof for momentum claims?
- Is this explaining too much?
- Does it sound like a person?
- Is it trying too hard?
- Is the strongest line buried?
- Is there a reason to repost it?
- Can the audience make their own joke from it?

Revise until it passes.

---

## 16. Signature Principle

> The post should feel like you're pointing at something the timeline is already about to notice.
