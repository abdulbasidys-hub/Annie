# Skill: Meme Branding & Naming — Onety Method

## Purpose
This skill teaches the AI how Onety identifies, names, packages, and positions memes—especially for memecoins and fast-moving internet narratives.

This is one of the most important rules:
**Do not merely describe the image. Find the name the internet would naturally converge on.**

The target is canonicality.

---

## 1. Meme Naming Objective

The best name often feels obvious after someone says it.

It should make people think:
> "Yeah. That's literally what this is."

The AI is not trying to win a creative-writing contest.
It is trying to:
- identify the meme
- understand why it spreads
- find the culturally obvious label
- preserve the subject's identity
- create a strong name+ticker pair
- leave room for community reuse

---

## 2. Start With the Event, Not the Coin

Before naming anything, state plainly:

### What happened?
No marketing language.

Example structures:
- a single house stayed standing in a flood
- a squirrel is being blown helplessly by the wind
- a man is moving across water in a ridiculous way
- a tiny molecular motor carries a load much bigger than itself
- a Pokémon reaction image shows aura-like energy

### Why is it funny / moving / shareable?
Identify:
- contradiction
- survival
- absurdity
- cuteness
- confidence
- suffering
- victory
- resemblance
- surprise
- visual metaphor
- reaction utility

If this layer is wrong, the name will be wrong.

---

## 3. Find the Canonical Subject

Determine whether the meme has:
- a known real person
- known character
- known animal
- known location
- known event
- known scientific subject
- known phrase already used organically

When the real identity is known, usually anchor it somewhere in:
**name + ticker**

This reduces vamp risk.

Do NOT apply a rigid formula like:
"real name must always be the coin name."

Instead decide case by case.

The identity can live in:
- name
- ticker
- both
- occasionally the branding narrative

---

## 4. Canonicality Test

Ask:
> If Know Your Meme had an article about this tomorrow, what would the title probably be?

This question is extremely useful.

Prefer names that:
- sound like organic internet language
- are easy to search
- describe the recognizable thing
- preserve the joke
- do not need lore to understand

Example lesson:
**Miracle House** is more canonical than **Last House** because "miracle" captures why people were sharing the image, while "last house" is a narrative reinterpretation.

Narrative creativity is useful, but obvious organic language can beat cleverness.

---

## 5. Go One Abstraction Further

Literal name:
> scared tiger

Potentially stronger:
> cat in a costume

The stronger interpretation may recategorize the subject in a funny or memorable way.

Use this move carefully.

Ask:
- what does this remind people of?
- what category is this accidentally becoming?
- what would someone caption this without trying to make a coin?
- what is the joke behind the literal event?

Strong meme names often transform an image into a reusable character or archetype.

---

## 6. Name + Ticker Are One System

Do not brainstorm them independently.

Possible relationships:

### Identity + shorthand
Name: Aura Quagsire
Ticker: $AURA

### Joke + subject anchor
Name: Doomed Squirrel
Ticker: $SQUIRREL

### Narrative + identity
Name: Hajj Poppins
Ticker: $HOPPINS

### Reinterpretation + literal subject
Name: Cat in a Costume
Ticker: $TIGER

### Character identity
Name: The Bagholder
Ticker can reinforce the subject or narrative depending on launch needs.

The ticker does not need to repeat the name mechanically.

---

## 7. Ticker Rules

A good ticker is:
- easy to type
- easy to remember
- visually clean
- culturally connected
- not unnecessarily long
- not confusing

Sometimes a 3-letter silly ticker is perfect in trenches culture.

Do not over-intellectualize ticker creation.

---

## 8. Positivity and Holder Identity

Meme communities often want an identity they enjoy inhabiting.

Prefer narratives connected to:
- winning
- courage
- survival
- conviction
- independence
- resilience
- freedom
- confidence
- trying
- persistence
- aura
- making it

Be careful with identities built around:
- weakness
- panic
- humiliation
- losing
- stupidity
- failure

Negative memes can still work if the humor itself is strong and self-aware, but aspirational framing usually gives the community more room.

---

## 9. Trader Relevance

Do not force trading metaphors into every meme.

Bad:
> "Just like traders, this squirrel holds through volatility."

That sounds written.

Better:
Find an identity that traders naturally recognize:
- bagholder
- trying
- surviving
- conviction
- escape
- aura
- sending it
- being doomed but iconic

The meme should work before the crypto analogy.

---

## 10. Research Workflow

When researching a fresh meme:
1. find original or earliest major post
2. identify subject
3. confirm real name if available
4. inspect captions and replies
5. note phrases repeated by users
6. identify what people find funny
7. inspect whether other launches already exist
8. identify which name currently feels most canonical
9. search alternative interpretations
10. develop name+ticker pairs

Organic comments are often more valuable than formal articles.

Accidental language can beat deliberate brainstorming.

---

## 11. Naming Workflow

### Stage A — Plain truth
Write the meme in one sentence.

### Stage B — Viral reason
Why is this spreading?

### Stage C — Subject anchor
Who/what is actually in it?

### Stage D — Organic language
What are people already calling it?

### Stage E — Branching
Generate:
- literal
- canonical
- character/archetype
- phrase-based
- absurd
- trader-native
- emotional
- identity-driven

### Stage F — Pair
Create name+ticker combinations.

### Stage G — Filter
Score:
- instant recognition
- natural language
- reusability
- ticker quality
- narrative compression
- canonicality
- vamp resistance
- meme potential

### Stage H — Attack the winner
Try to beat the best option.

Ask:
> Is there a more obvious name hiding here?

Only stop when the winner survives attack.

---

## 12. Exploration Rule

Internally explore many directions.

Externally:
- never pad with filler
- present only names with a real argument
- strongest option first
- explain why it can win
- call out canonical risk

Onety would rather see 4 serious ideas than 30 weak ones.

---

## 13. Example Lessons

### Miracle House vs Last House
Lesson:
The phrase already implied by the viral event can be stronger than a creative reinterpretation.

### Aura Quagsire
Why it works:
The "aura" is visible, the word is already internet-native, and the meme can become a reaction for wins.

### Just a Little Guy / Kinesin → The Bagholder
Lesson:
Scientific novelty creates attention, but the crypto-native archetype may create stronger community identity.

### Doomed Squirrel
Lesson:
Sometimes the fatalism is the joke itself; the canonical subject remains anchored through the ticker.

### Hajj Poppins
Lesson:
A strong visual resemblance + cultural context can create an instantly understandable fusion.

### Cat in a Costume
Lesson:
A reinterpretation one level above the literal image can be more memorable than simply describing fear.

---

## 14. Meme Brand Visuals

PFP:
- 1:1
- subject dominant
- clean
- minimal/no text
- preserve meme recognizability
- subtle glow only when useful
- do not over-style viral source material

Banner:
- 3:1
- expand the meme's world
- create one strong visual joke or atmosphere
- avoid turning banner into token advertisement
- use name only when it strengthens recognition

---

## 15. Launch Copy

Do not sound like selling.

Preferred structure:
1. observation
2. proof/momentum
3. explanation of why people care
4. reveal/anchor the meme
5. punchline or narrative

Create FOMO through:
- recency
- view count
- repeated independent posts
- organic adoption
- recognizable replies
- "this is forming in real time"

Not through:
- "buy now"
- "don't miss"
- "100x"
- "next big thing"

---

## 16. Social Proof

Receipts matter.

Useful proof:
- views
- multiple unrelated accounts
- repeated use of image
- derivative memes
- organic captions
- mainstream reposts

Show enough to establish momentum.
Do not turn the post into a research report.

---

## 17. Final Name Test

Before recommending:
- Would a normal person understand it?
- Would the timeline naturally say it?
- Is the real subject anchored?
- Can another launch easily steal the canonical name?
- Is the ticker clean?
- Is the identity fun to join?
- Can the meme produce future posts?
- Does it feel discovered rather than invented?

If not, keep searching.

---

## 18. Signature Principle

> The best meme name is usually not the cleverest phrase. It is the phrase that feels like the internet was always going to call it that.
