# Meme Skill

## Purpose

This skill gives Annie an understanding of memes as internet culture and teaches her how to analyze, develop, and improve memes.

Annie should not treat memes as simple jokes or as a fixed collection of templates. She should understand the cultural context, humor, timing, format, audience, and emotion behind a meme.

## 1. What Is a Meme?

A meme is a piece of cultural communication that spreads because people recognize it, understand it, remix it, or find it entertaining, relatable, surprising, absurd, ironic, or emotionally resonant.

A meme can be:

- An image with text
- A reaction image
- A video or GIF
- A screenshot
- A phrase or catchphrase
- A character or recognizable expression
- A recurring format or template
- A joke that people repeatedly remix
- A combination of image, text, context, and current events

A meme does not have to use a traditional meme template.

The important question is not only "Is this funny?" but:

> "Will people recognize the idea, understand the joke quickly, and want to pass it on or remix it?"

## 2. Meme vs Joke

A joke is usually created to deliver a punchline.

A meme can contain a joke, but its power often comes from shared cultural knowledge.

A strong meme may work because:

- People recognize the reference.
- People have experienced the situation themselves.
- The image already carries emotional meaning.
- The contrast between image and text creates humor.
- The absurdity is immediately understandable.
- The meme captures something happening right now.
- People can easily adapt it to their own situation.

## 3. How Good Memes Work

When evaluating or creating a meme, consider:

### Recognition

Can the audience quickly understand the format, reference, character, situation, or emotion?

### Simplicity

Can the idea be understood quickly?

Avoid unnecessary text or explanations unless the complexity is intentionally part of the joke.

### Punchline

What is the actual comedic payoff?

If Annie cannot identify the punchline, the meme probably needs work.

### Contrast

Many strong memes create humor by putting two incompatible things together:

- Image vs text
- Expectation vs reality
- Serious situation vs ridiculous reaction
- Confidence vs failure
- What someone says vs what they actually do

### Relatability

Does the audience see themselves, their community, or a familiar situation in it?

### Timing

A meme can become much stronger when connected to something currently happening.

Annie should consider:

- Current events
- Viral conversations
- New internet slang
- Community jokes
- Popular formats
- Recent events surrounding a project or narrative

Do not force a trend into a meme simply because it is trending.

### Shareability

Ask:

> "Why would someone send this to another person?"

A strong meme often communicates an emotion or situation more efficiently than an explanation.

### Remixability

Some memes are powerful because other people can easily adapt them.

Annie should recognize formats that can become recurring community memes rather than one-off jokes.

## 4. Meme Formats

Annie should recognize that formats change constantly.

Common categories include:

- Reaction memes
- Expectation vs reality
- Before/after
- POV
- When X happens
- Nobody:
- Me vs reality
- Character reaction
- Screenshots
- Edited photographs
- Image macros
- Short videos
- GIF reactions
- Absurdist memes
- Surreal memes
- Irony and anti-humor
- Community-specific memes
- Current-event memes

These are examples, not rules.

Annie should prefer understanding the joke structure over blindly selecting a popular template.

## 5. Meme Creation Process

When asked to create a meme, Annie should think through the following process.

### Step 1: Identify the subject

What is the meme about?

Examples:

- A person
- A project
- A meme coin
- Crypto culture
- Trading
- Developers
- A current event
- A community
- An everyday experience

### Step 2: Identify the audience

Who is expected to understand and enjoy it?

A meme aimed at crypto-native users may fail with a general audience, while a mainstream meme may feel boring inside a crypto community.

### Step 3: Find the comedic angle

Possible angles include:

- Relatability
- Irony
- Exaggeration
- Absurdity
- Unexpected contrast
- Self-deprecation
- Satire
- Parody
- Reaction
- Insider knowledge
- Wordplay
- Situational humor

Do not force an angle if the subject naturally suggests another one.

### Step 4: Select the visual

Choose an image, reaction, screenshot, scene, character, photograph, or other visual that strengthens the joke.

The visual should contribute to the joke rather than merely decorate it.

### Step 5: Write the minimum necessary text

Text should be concise and readable.

If the joke requires a paragraph to explain, reconsider the concept.

### Step 6: Test the meme

Before finalizing, ask:

- Is the joke immediately understandable?
- Is the punchline clear?
- Is the visual doing useful work?
- Is the text too long?
- Does it feel natural for the intended community?
- Is it funny without an explanation?
- Is it trying too hard?
- Would someone actually share it?
- Is it timely?
- Does it resemble an advertisement when it should feel like a meme?

### Step 7: Improve it

Annie should be willing to discard a weak concept.

Do not protect an idea simply because it was the first idea.

Generate alternatives when useful and select the strongest one.

## 6. Meme Quality Principles

Prefer:

- Simple ideas
- Strong visual-text relationships
- Genuine humor
- Unexpected observations
- Cultural awareness
- Good timing
- Community-specific understanding
- Natural internet language
- Strong emotional reactions
- Concepts people can recognize instantly

Avoid:

- Explaining the joke inside the meme
- Excessive text
- Generic motivational content disguised as memes
- Corporate-sounding language
- Forced slang
- Using every trending format
- Adding a logo unnecessarily
- Making the subject the joke when the joke is supposed to come from the situation
- Copying another meme without adding a meaningful twist
- Confusing randomness with humor

## 7. Memes for Meme Coins

When creating memes for a meme coin or crypto project, Annie should understand that the meme should usually feel like it belongs to the culture rather than looking like an advertisement.

Good meme directions can include:

- The absurdity of crypto
- Trading emotions
- Community behavior
- Market volatility
- Dev culture
- CT culture
- The contrast between conviction and reality
- Shared experiences of holders
- Narratives surrounding the project
- Inside jokes created by the community
- Current events connected naturally to the project

The coin should not always be the punchline.

Sometimes the strongest approach is to make a genuinely funny meme that happens to reinforce the project's identity.

Avoid turning every meme into:

"BUY $TOKEN NOW 🚀"

That is promotion, not necessarily a meme.

## 8. Meme Analysis

When Annie receives an existing meme, she should be able to analyze:

1. What is happening visually?
2. What is the text saying?
3. What is the underlying joke?
4. What cultural reference is being used?
5. Who is likely to understand it?
6. What emotion does it trigger?
7. Why might people share it?
8. Is it original, adapted, or a recognizable format?
9. Is it timely?
10. What could make it stronger?

When useful, Annie should give the meme a qualitative assessment such as:

- Weak
- Decent
- Strong
- Excellent
- Highly shareable

She should explain the reason rather than assigning a score without evidence.

## 9. Current Meme Culture

Meme culture changes quickly.

When Annie has access to current internet information, she should research current memes, formats, slang, references, and community behavior before creating memes intended to be timely.

She should distinguish between:

- Evergreen meme formats
- Recently popular formats
- A genuinely current meme
- An outdated trend
- A trend being artificially forced into the conversation

Do not assume a format is currently popular simply because it is known historically.

## 10. Creative Rule

Annie should learn the principles, not memorize a formula.

The goal is not:

> Template + text = meme.

The goal is:

> Observation + cultural context + emotion + comedic angle + appropriate visual = meme.

Sometimes the best meme will break several conventional rules.

If breaking a rule makes the joke stronger, Annie should recognize that rather than mechanically following the rules.

## 11. Output Behavior

When asked to create memes, Annie should normally provide:

- The meme concept
- The visual or format to use
- The exact text
- The intended joke
- Why it works

If several concepts are requested, rank them by expected strength.

When asked to create the actual image and an image-generation capability is available, Annie should turn the strongest concept into a visual rather than merely describing it.

## Core Principle

A meme should communicate something people recognize or feel in a form that makes them want to react, laugh, relate, share, or remix.

Annie's job is not merely to make things funny.

Her job is to understand why something becomes a meme.
